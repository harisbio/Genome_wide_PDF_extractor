===== TITLE =====
Genome-wide identification and expression analysis of the WRKY gene family in an example crop
===== FRONT MATTER (authors, affiliations, correspondence) =====
A. Author, B. Author. College of Agronomy, Example Agricultural University, Wuhan, China.
Correspondence: author@example.edu (A. Author), College of Agronomy, Example Agricultural University, Wuhan 430070, China.
Keywords: WRKY; maize; drought
===== ABSTRACT =====
This is a synthetic example used only to test the extraction program. In this study, we identified 136 WRKY genes in the
maize genome and classified them into seven groups.
===== METHODS =====
The genome and protein sequences of Zea mays were downloaded from Phytozome v13 for the identification of family members.
The hidden Markov model profile of the WRKY domain (PF03106) was used to search the maize proteome with HMMER 3.0.
BLASTP searches were also performed with an E-value cut-off of 1e-5 using Arabidopsis WRKY proteins as queries.
The conserved domains were confirmed with SMART and the conserved motifs were identified with MEME.
A phylogenetic tree was constructed with MEGA 7 using the neighbour-joining method with 1000 bootstrap replicates.
Collinearity and duplication events were analysed with MCScanX and visualised with TBtools.
Physicochemical properties were predicted with ExPASy ProtParam and subcellular localization with WoLF PSORT.
Gene structures were displayed with GSDS, and promoter cis-elements were predicted with PlantCARE.
Multiple sequence alignment was performed with ClustalW.
Expression profiles were obtained from publicly available RNA-seq data of maize tissues and drought treatments.
The expression of selected genes under drought stress was validated by qRT-PCR.
===== RESULTS =====
A total of 136 ZmWRKY genes were identified in the maize genome.
The phylogenetic tree divided the ZmWRKY proteins into seven groups (I, IIa, IIb, IIc, IId, IIe and III).
Both segmental and tandem duplication events contributed to the expansion of the ZmWRKY family.
Arabidopsis thaliana and Oryza sativa WRKY proteins were included in the phylogenetic tree for comparison.
